## Full description

Every bar of metal has a heat it wants — and the game never tells you what it is.

You are handed a workshop, a commission, and a purse that runs out faster than
the shifts do. Set the bellows, hire apprentices, cut tooling, decide how hard
to drive the shop — then stand at the anvil and swing. Every tap is one blow of
your own hammer, and one pair of arms only has so many in it.

At the end of the shift the foreman tells you what happened. Not a number: a
sentence.

  "The bar came off the fire dull and stiff. It fought the hammer, and most of
  it cracked."   — too cold.

  "Sparks everywhere. That was not metal moving — that was metal burning."
   — too hot.

  "It moved like butter under the hammer. Whatever you did to that fire, do it
  again."   — you have found it.

That is the whole game: ten notches on the bellows, one of them right, and a
foreman who will only ever tell you which way to move.

• **625 commissions.** 25 cities of smiths, 25 workshops each, across four
  difficulty tiers. Sheffield's coke is nearly free; Norilsk is minus forty
  outside the door; in Chittagong the steel comes off a beached ship and eats
  every die you own.

• **Eight alloys**, from forgiving mild steel to meteoric iron that is worth a
  year of gates and ruins itself if you are one notch out.

• **Heat is never banked.** The fire dies overnight, in every workshop, however
  rich you are — and every hammer on the metal drags it back down while you
  work. It is the one thing money cannot buy ahead.

• **The commissions are measured, not guessed.** A family of reference smiths
  works each exact workshop and the target is a share of what the best of them
  delivers. No workshop can ever ask for more than it can give.

• **The same seed always replays the same commission**, so a workshop you lost
  is a puzzle you can learn rather than a dice roll.

• **The guild.** Hold five cities and you stop working shop by shop: open
  depots, buy bar stock by the ton, and station journeymen on workshops you
  already hold. They can do everything you can do except swing your hammer.

• **Plays entirely offline.** No account, no network, no tracking, nothing to
  collect. One phone, one anvil.
